# MusashiMap — Book II / WATER — Geographic Research Pass D

Status: **completed for the current C/D queue**

Purpose: investigate every C/D item from Pass C with the explicit goal of promoting confidence where evidence permits, while refusing to invent coordinates or historical continuity.

## Rules applied

- The novel corpus in `data/source/book2/` remains the narrative authority.
- External sources are used only to identify or test real-world geography/history.
- A modern site is not treated as period-identical merely because the name matches.
- Exact coordinates require site-specific evidence; an area-level identification may legitimately remain an area/representative point.
- Historical compatibility is tracked separately from geographic identity.
- Fictional/narrative-only places remain unmapped when no defensible real-world identification exists.

## Detailed findings

### Yoshioka School — promoted C → B

The National Diet Library catalogue contains a local-history audiovisual record titled **"武蔵が歩いた道 吉岡道場の跡を尋ねて 西洞院四条通 西の辻"**. Its description says it visits the area of Kyoto where the Yoshioka dojo is believed to have stood, specifically around Nishinotoin and Shijō. The catalogue gives a geographic scope in present-day Shimogyo-ku. This is substantially stronger than generic web claims, but the wording still indicates a reconstructed/claimed location rather than a surviving, archaeologically fixed building footprint. Therefore B is the honest ceiling for the present evidence.

Source: National Diet Library Search / Digital Okayama local-history record.

### Hannya Plain — promoted C → A at area level

Historical geographic material places Hannya-no around Hannyaji/Narasaka. A historical transcription states that the area around Hannyaji was called Hannyazaka or Hannyano and separately describes Hannya-no Gozanmai. A Nara Prefecture historical-material publication likewise identifies the cemetery called Hannyano Gozanmai below Hannyazaka. This establishes the historical toponym strongly enough for an area-level anchor.

Important limitation: **A does not mean an exact battlefield coordinate.** The narrative's combat scene should use the historically identified Hannya area, not an invented point inside it.

### Hannya Hill — promoted C → A as historical slope/corridor

The same historical geography identifies Hannyazaka as a named slope in the Narasaka/Hannyaji area. The modern road network is not assumed to preserve the exact seventeenth-century geometry. The appropriate production object is therefore a named slope/corridor or representative point.

### Yagyū dōjō — C → B

The present Masakizaka Kenzen Dojo is explicitly modern: Nara City tourism dates its establishment to 1965 and explains that parts of its structure were moved from other buildings. Local historical material says the dojo tradition/name descends from the Masakizaka dojo associated with Yagyū Jūbei, while the original training activity was within the jinya. This is enough to reject the current building as a literal Book-II marker, but not enough to locate the earlier training space independently. B is therefore the correct ceiling.

### Yagyū mountain house — C → B

The narrative gives a very strong internal relationship: the mountain house is a small residence inside the Yagyū castle grounds, deeper within the complex. Historical sources identify the Yagyū Castle/residential area, but no authoritative source establishes a separately preserved house corresponding to the novel's mountain house. We can therefore locate the parent area confidently while keeping the child location unpinned.

### Shin'indō — C → B

The narrative explicitly makes Shin'indō a building within the Yagyū compound used to receive travelling swordsmen. External evidence supports the parent castle/residence but does not provide a surviving independent footprint. The correct model is a child location inheriting the castle's geometry/anchor.

### Tsukigase–Iga back road — C → B

The existence of historical road corridors around Yagyū is strongly documented by Nara City and the Yagyū tourism association. The exact line from the Book-II scene toward Iga is not established as a canonical surviving road. Therefore B as a corridor is appropriate; an exact polyline would be invented unless reconstructed from explicit waypoints.

### Kūyadō — C → B, with a major historical warning

Kūyadō is unquestionably a real historical religious site. However, the present site is not the correct seventeenth-century coordinate: authoritative Kyoto sources state that the current site dates from the Kan'ei period, while the older Kūyadō was associated with Sanjō Kushige. The historical-place encyclopedia identifies the original location as present-day Imashinzaike-nishimachi. This is enough to improve the identity to B, but **not** enough to use the modern temple point for the Book-II scene.

### Rokuamida — C → B as a contextual concept

The official Kyoto tourism source confirms that "Rakuyō Six Amida" is a pilgrimage consisting of six distinct temples. Therefore a generic `rokuamida` point is geographically incorrect. The correct representation is a contextual reference or a relation to the specific named temple when the narrative supplies one. The confidence upgrade is about understanding the entity, not inventing a coordinate.

### Abura Hill — C → A at area level

Historical place-name evidence and current Nara tourism mapping both preserve Aburazaka/Aburasaka as a real Nara locality/slope. The exact historical street geometry is not assumed identical to today's roads, but the toponym itself is secure.

### Wataya — remains D

No authoritative historical source found in this pass ties the novel's Wataya inn to a surviving or documented modern location in Yagyū. Unrelated modern establishments named Wataya were deliberately rejected. This is a textbook case where the correct answer is **D / unmapped**, not a guessed coordinate.

### Yomogi — remains D

The novel establishes Yomogi as a Kyoto teahouse, but no sufficiently reliable historical geographic source was found that identifies it with a real establishment. Modern uses of the name are not evidence. It remains narrative-only.

### Unnamed Kyoto inn — remains D

There is no proper-name or landmark evidence capable of identifying it. It must remain unmapped.

### Hongandō and Ōzōin — remain C

The searches did not reach the evidentiary threshold required for a production coordinate. These names may correspond to real religious sites, but an unambiguous scene-level identification was not established. They remain research candidates rather than map points.

## Critical conclusion

The research goal was to promote C/D items to A where evidence permits. The result is **not** "everything is A", because several locations are narrative-specific or historically displaced and no responsible source provides an exact identification. Promoting those anyway would violate the project's anti-invention rule.

The most important production consequences are:

1. **Hannya Plain/Hannya Hill can now be represented geographically at area/slope level.**
2. **Aburazaka can be represented at area/route level.**
3. **Yoshioka School should use an area-level anchor around Nishinotoin–Shijō, not an invented exact dojo footprint.**
4. **Yagyū dōjō, Shin'indō and Sekishūsai's mountain house should be modeled as sub-locations of the Yagyū complex rather than falsely independent modern points.**
5. **Kūyadō requires special temporal handling: the present temple location is not automatically the Book-II location.**
6. **Rokuamida must not become a fake single marker.**
7. **Wataya, Yomogi and the unnamed inn remain unmapped.**
