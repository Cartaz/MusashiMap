# MusashiMap — Book II / WATER — Pass C

Pass C closes the remaining primary-text verification for Book II Chapters 1, 9, 10 and 11 and records the production blockers. No production JSON is modified by this document.

## Chapter 1 — final source verification

The complete repository source for `chapter1-the-yoshioka-school.txt` has now been checked. The final portion confirms:

- Matahachi remains at the Yomogi Teahouse while Okō, Akemi and Seijūrō/Tōji leave for the riverside entertainment district.
- Matahachi attempts to leave, realizes he has no money, returns to search Okō's rooms, and remains in the Yomogi context.
- A messenger from the Yoshioka School arrives and explicitly reports that Miyamoto Musashi has appeared at the school and refuses to leave until he can face the Young Master.
- This closes the Chapter 1 state with Musashi physically at the Yoshioka School and the Yomogi group still in Kyoto.

The previously pending Chapter 1 tail is therefore no longer a blocker.

## Chapter 9 — final verification

The repository source confirms the following sequence:

1. Sekishūsai and Otsū are together at the secluded mountain house in Yagyū territory.
2. Shōda Kizaemon returns from his errand concerning the visiting Yoshioka party.
3. The Yoshioka party is staying at the Wataya inn; Denshichirō is the identified principal member.
4. Sekishūsai sends Otsū to the Wataya with a letter and white peony as a diplomatic substitute for meeting Denshichirō.
5. Otsū travels from Koyagyū Castle to the Wataya on horseback and returns to the castle.
6. The peony is passed to Kocha at the inn. Musashi examines its cut and reproduces it with his sword, realizing the original cut was made by an exceptional swordsman.
7. Musashi sends Jōtarō to the Yagyū castle with a letter and the cut stem asking who made the cut.
8. Jōtarō enters the castle grounds and delivers the message to Kizaemon.
9. Kizaemon, Murata, Kimura and Debuchi identify the flower as one cut by Sekishūsai and infer that the sender is Miyamoto Musashi.
10. The Yagyū retainers invite Musashi to the Shin'indō for food and conversation.
11. Jōtarō has a confrontation with the castle dog Tarō, resulting in the injuries that open Chapter 10.

Important location distinction: the Wataya inn, Koyagyū Castle/Main House, the Yagyū dōjō and Sekishūsai's mountain house remain distinct narrative locations.

## Chapter 10 — final verification

The repository source confirms:

- Jōtarō returns to the Wataya with scratches after his confrontation with Tarō; Kocha treats him at the inn.
- Musashi leaves the Wataya and proceeds to the main gate of Koyagyū Castle.
- Musashi is admitted on Kizaemon's invitation and is taken to the Shin'indō.
- The Shin'indō is a scholarly/library building within the castle complex.
- Musashi meets Kizaemon, Kimura Sukekurō, Debuchi Magobei and Murata Yozō.
- The four Yagyū retainers test Musashi socially and intellectually through the discussion of the peony cut.
- Tarō's abnormal barking interrupts the gathering.
- The dog is found dead with Jōtarō beside it; Jōtarō admits killing it as revenge after being attacked.
- Musashi intervenes when Jōtarō is threatened, escalating the confrontation with the castle retainers.
- Kizaemon temporarily prevents the crowd from killing them and the four senior retainers confront Musashi directly.
- Musashi refuses suicide or passive imprisonment and states explicitly that his objective is to meet Yagyū Sekishūsai.
- The confrontation develops into a four-against-one fight around the castle grounds.
- Otsū's flute breaks Musashi's concentration at the critical moment, allowing him to escape.
- Musashi flees toward/over the inner castle moat and disappears into the darkness.

The chapter therefore ends with Musashi still in the Koyagyū/Yagyū castle environment but physically escaping the immediate confrontation.

## Chapter 11 — final verification

The repository source confirms:

- Musashi hides in the castle moat after escaping the retainers.
- He spends the night searching the castle grounds for Sekishūsai's mountain house.
- He reaches the secluded mountain-house gate near daybreak and recognizes the character of the retreat from its landscaping and architecture.
- He drinks and washes at the brook running through the mountain-house grounds.
- He decides not to force entry after understanding the character of Sekishūsai's retirement.
- Otsū arrives at the mountain house and her flute is the sound Musashi has been hearing.
- Otsū and Jōtarō are both searching for Musashi.
- Jōtarō finds Musashi's hand towel, which identifies his recent presence at the brook.
- Otsū and Jōtarō pursue Musashi after briefly seeing him, but he escapes toward the wooded foothills.
- They reach the back road running from Tsukigase toward Iga and lose sight of him at the wooded foothills.
- Takuan subsequently encounters Otsū and Jōtarō and walks with them through the Yagyū-area roads.
- Takuan has an established prior relationship with the Yagyū family and with Sekishūsai, and later parts of the chapter explain the correspondence that brought him toward Koyagyū.
- Otsū ultimately chooses to continue following her own path rather than remain in Koyagyū.
- Jōtarō chooses to continue following Musashi.

The chapter therefore closes the Yagyū sequence with Musashi moving away from the castle/household and Otsū/Jōtarō departing by a separate narrative path.

## Continuity result: Chapters 1–11

The complete continuity chain is now sufficiently verified for research normalization:

`Kyoto / Yoshioka School → Kyoto / Yomogi → Kiyomizudera/Hongandō → Kyoto inn → Nara/Hōzōin → Hannya Plain → Koyagyū/Yagyū → Wataya → Koyagyū Castle → castle moat / mountain house → Yagyū-area roads`

This is a narrative sequence, not a reconstructed historical itinerary. Where the novel does not explicitly establish the route between two locations, the production data must retain that uncertainty.
