## Hōzōin caution

The Hōzōin-ryū official history states that the tradition originated at Hōzōin, a sub-temple of Kōfuku-ji in Nara. The modern Hōzōin-ryū organization must not be substituted for the historical temple location. This is an identification of the historical/narrative place, not a claim that the modern dojo is the novel's location.

Source: Hōzōin-ryū official history — https://hozoin.org/rekisi/

## Kōfuku-ji / Sarusawa cluster

Nara's official tourism authority identifies Kōfuku-ji as the major temple complex in central Nara and documents its historical importance in Yamato. Sarusawa Pond is documented by the same authority as a distinct site beside Kōfuku-ji. These should remain separate location records where the novel distinguishes them.

Sources:
- Nara Travelers Guide, Kōfuku-ji: https://narashikanko.or.jp/en/spot/detail_10029.html
- Nara City Tourism Association, Sarusawa-related site: https://narashikanko.or.jp/spot/detail_10060.html

## Yagyū / Koyagyū

The existing research pass already established Yagyū as the geographic area and Koyagyū Castle as a distinct site. The Japanese Historical Place Names POI dataset provides a coordinate for the Yagyū Castle site. We should use the historical-site coordinate as the representative map point rather than inventing a coordinate for the fictionalized castle interior.

Source: Geoshape / Japanese Historical Place Names POI dataset, Yagyū Castle site.

## Confidence policy

No coordinate is promoted to production solely because a modern name looks similar to the novel's name. Each production location must have:

1. a narrative identification from the Book II corpus;
2. a modern/historical geographic identification;
3. a confidence level;
4. a documented reason for the chosen representative point or area;
5. a note when the modern site differs from the historical site described by the novel.
